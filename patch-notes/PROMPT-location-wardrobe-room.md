## It carries more of this arc than anything else in it

It is where the four came from, it is where the inventory hangs, and **the arc ends in it**.
That is the Warehouse argument exactly — section 6.1 built a master because eleven images
happened in one building and independent generations would each have been internally
consistent and clearly not the same room. This room has the same exposure and a harder job.

## The reference exists to plant a door before it matters

Volume 3: *“It is here. Not in the wires. In the room, behind the trunks.”* Volume 4:
*“Behind the trunks, in the north wall, there was a door that didn’t appear on any drawing in
any county file. It was about four and a half feet tall and it was standing open.”*

**So the door has to already be in the room, shut, before anyone looks at it.** If the
reference leaves it out and a Volume 4 plate adds it, the biggest reveal in the arc arrives
from nowhere and the reader is entitled to feel cheated. The prompt puts it in the north wall,
four and a half feet tall, mostly behind the trunks, painted the wall colour — **findable and
not the subject**. It is the same discipline as the empty corner the Warehouse master has to
leave for Lena’s desk in Volume 4: the reference shows it free, the later plate shows it used.

**This is state A.** Trunk seven open and the small door shut. Volume 4 is state B, with the
door standing open — and beyond it a second room the reference does not show at all.

## Two things the negative block forces, and one of them is free

- **The trunk numbers cannot be drawn.** No numerals in an image, ever. That looks like a
  problem, because trunk seven matters enormously — and it is not one, because **trunk seven
  identifies itself by being the only open trunk in the row**. The white paint stays as paint.
- **The inventory sheet is unreadable**, the same as the OPEN sign in the gym. Two hundred and
  six items live in the prose.

## Lit plainly, on purpose

They arrive with flashlights and Pip does not use one. That is the *plate’s* lighting, not the
reference’s — a reference has to show the room, so this one has a working fixture and reads
clearly everywhere. Individual plates can light it however the beat wants.
