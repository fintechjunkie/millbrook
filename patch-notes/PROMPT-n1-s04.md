## What to keep from the current plate

It is a good picture and only one thing is wrong with it. Milo on the floor against the tub with
the beanie and the red lenses still on and a clear grey-green sickness in his skin; his mother on
the rim beside him mid-anecdote with one hand lifted and the other resting flat on the bath, not
touching him; the tiled bathroom; the lit room at night; the close two-shot at floor height. All
of that is the plate and none of it should move.

## The one thing

**She was the wrong woman, and that came out of my prompt rather than out of the story.** The
previous version invented a Black woman in her forties with warm brown skin and a patterned
headscarf. That contradicts her son: Milo is fair to medium skinned with dark reddish-brown hair.
She now has a canonical sheet built to make him make sense — fair skin, freckles, strong dark
brows, heavy-lidded dark eyes, and short black hair with a dark fringe.

**She wears no hat in this plate.** The flowered hat on her sheet is what the town would know her
by. This is three in the morning in a bathroom, so she is in a dressing gown with bare feet.

**She is still not worried.** Milo never gets sick and has got sick, and what she does about it
is tell him a story he has probably heard before — amused, unhurried, mid-sentence, plainly
enjoying herself. That is the whole point of the beat and it is what the current plate already
gets right.
