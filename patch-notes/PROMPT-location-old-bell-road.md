### The shoulders are load-bearing, and this is the same class of thing as the gym

> *“They tried to drive around her, so she walked over and stood in front of the truck again.
> They backed up and tried the other side, so she walked over and stood in front of it again.
> Old Bell Road stayed closed for six hours because one girl in armor who didn’t want to fight
> anybody wouldn’t be anywhere else.”*

**That beat only works on a road of one specific width.** Too narrow and there is nothing to
try; too wide and the truck simply drives around and the six hours never happen. So the
reference gives each shoulder just enough gravel to attempt it and a ditch beyond that is
obviously undriveable — and the two sides must match, because the truck tries both.

Same reasoning as the bag and the water cooler eleven feet apart in Benny’s gym, and the fence
turning around the outside of the dumpster pad.

### The checkpoint is furniture, not a barricade

Dunphy has this stop *“and he didn’t mind it, because people kept bringing him things. Two
doughnuts and a folding chair, so far.”* Nothing about it should look fortified. Two barriers,
two cones, a camp chair and a folding table on the gravel, and the road itself open.

The chair matters: it was **brought to him by somebody from the town**, which is the whole joke
of the occupation. It is a domestic folding chair, not military issue.
