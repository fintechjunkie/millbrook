## His height is arithmetic, and it explains a door

The prose never gives his size — but it walks him through something it does measure.
*"Behind the trunks, in the north wall, there was a door… about four and a half feet tall"*,
and at the end, *"Chet stepped through and pulled the door shut behind him."*

**He steps through it. He does not stoop through it.** So he is under four and a half feet,
and the sheet puts him at about four — which quietly answers a question the reader has been
carrying since Volume 4 opened, because it makes that absurd little door **his door**, built
to his measure by whatever built it. Do not draw him man-sized.

## He looks like Pip, and that is the point

Pip is *"a fully robotic humanoid girl… pale warm grey… sandy blonde hair… a circular
mechanical port beside her ear."* Chet is pale bone-grey with straw-blond hair and a round
port on each side of his head. **That resemblance is deliberate and must survive.**

The arc's last real beat is *"He's the one who wanted to see how you all turned out." Chet
looked at Pip when he said it.* A family resemblance is carrying that line, and designing it
away to avoid a clash would cost the ending something it cannot get back.

They separate on everything else, and the separation is total:

| | Chet | Pip |
|---|---|---|
| Face | a metal box, blank below the eye band, **no mouth and no nose** | a girl's face — eyes, brows, nose, purple lips |
| Build | four feet, squat, riveted, spring neck | tall, slim, straight, long-limbed |
| Wear | nothing, ever | red bandana, goggles, red neckerchief |
| Condition | a hundred repairs old, dented, rubbed through | clean, matte, exact |

## What the prose gives him, and it is all character

- **He is eleven hundred years old and he is not the clever one.** *"I'm slow. I'm eleven
  hundred years old and I have never once been the smart one."* So: worn out, patient,
  hangdog. Nothing about him should read as powerful or sinister.
- **He reads badly and knows it.** *"I read a file that said her hearing was zero and
  shouldn't be. I didn't read the other things about her, because nobody had written the
  other things down."* The whole arc happens because he did his best with a thin file.
- **His eyes do something that is not quite a blink.** So the eye panels must be able to
  change — hence lit panels with a movable pupil dot rather than glass lenses.
- **He sweeps broken glass into a square of paper towel**, and *watching a machine do that was
  the saddest thing any of them had ever seen*. He should look like something that would.
- **He is a little defensive about his work.** *"They came out good, though. Didn't they come
  out good?"*

## Two notes on the source art

- **The supplied image stops at the chest, so the legs are invented here** — short, plain and
  segmented in the same dark steel as the shoulder joints, with flat oval feet. Everything
  above the waist is transcription. If the legs come back wrong, they are the cheap part.
- **Benny also has spiked platinum hair.** A four-foot dented robot and a fifty-five-year-old
  gym owner will not be confused, and they never share a scene.

## [ ] The man in the coat

Save as: **`char-the-man-canonical.png`** · token `{{CHAR:THE_MAN}}` · aspect 3:4

## This one is transcription, not invention

He is the most precisely described new character in the arc, and the supplied art already
matches the prose almost line for line:

> *"a small figure in a black wool coat with three orange buttons.*
> *He was very short. Round glasses, no hair, and the face of a serious child who had been*
> *sitting at the same desk for a long, long time. He had his hands in his coat pockets."*

Three buttons, and the art has three. **So the only real job here is not to lose anything** —
the count, the pockets, the glasses, and the specific quality of the face, which is a grown
man wearing a child's seriousness. Not a child. Not whimsical. Not sinister.

## He is on screen for six sentences and never speaks

That is the whole performance, and every line of it is restraint:

> *"He looked up, and he looked at them for about two seconds, and he wasn't angry and he*
> *wasn't surprised and he didn't look caught. He took his time about it."*

> *"Then he looked at Pip and nodded once, the way a man nods at somebody who has finally*
> *shown up to a meeting he scheduled years ago."*

**So the sheet is emphatically neutral.** He is not mid-nod and he is not looking up. Every
canonical reference in this project is a figure doing nothing — the rule that kept Ida's wave
out of her sheet — and it matters more here than anywhere, because the nod is the character
and it belongs to the plate.

## The token is provisional

He has **no name in the manuscript**. `{{CHAR:THE_MAN}}` and `char-the-man-canonical.png` are
placeholders chosen so nothing invented leaks into canon by the back door. If he gets a name,
renaming one file and one roster row is trivial — say the word.

## His room is a separate job, and it is not small

> *"Bookshelves, or file drawers, rows and rows of them in low gold light, going up much*
> *further than the ceiling above their heads had any business allowing."*

Plus one wooden chair much too big for him, with his feet nowhere near the floor. That is the
last image of the arc and it is the only impossible space in either arc so far. It wants its
own reference and its own conversation.
