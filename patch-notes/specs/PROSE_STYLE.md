# Prose style, as revised by the author

Derived from the author's own edits to Volume 1 spreads 1 to 6 on 2026-07-29, by diffing them
against the original draft. These are descriptive rather than invented: every rule below is a
pattern the author applied repeatedly and consistently. Volumes 2, 3 and 4 are still in the
original register and should be brought to this one.

The summary in the author's words: *less choppy, fewer fragments, more strung-together
sentences, a little simpler language at times.*

---

## 1. Fragments become clauses

The original leaned on stacked fragments for rhythm. The revision joins them into sentences.

> Same yellow siding. Same chain link fence. Same ceramic frog by the front steps with one
> chipped ear.

becomes

> It was the same yellow siding, the same chain link fence and the same ceramic frog by the
> front steps that she remembered.

Same move, three more times:

- *Forum posts. Deleted news clips. Reddit threads with three replies.* → *…filled the screen
  with forum posts, deleted news clips, and Reddit threads with three replies.*
- *Two coffees. A paper bag with something greasy in it.* → *He was holding two coffees and a
  paper bag with something greasy in it.*
- *Nine months of work. Most of it junk.* → *It reflected nine months of work with most of it
  leading nowhere.*

**Where fragments survive:** short beats of physical action still stand alone. *Then he tapped
the screen. Once. With a single finger.* was left untouched, and so was *Not hiding. Just
sitting.* The rule is that fragments used for **emphasis** stay and fragments used as a
**list** get joined.

## 2. Adjacent sentences get a connective

Where the original set two sentences side by side and let the reader infer the join, the
revision states it: *and*, *but*, *because*, *before*, *so*.

> He'd noticed three days ago. He hadn't mentioned it yet, because Milo would ask what was at
> the center, and Owen didn't have an answer, and Owen hated not having answers.

becomes

> He'd noticed the pattern three days ago and hadn't mentioned it yet because Milo would ask
> what was at the center. Owen didn't have an answer yet and Owen hated not having answers.

Note it also breaks the long chain of *and*s into two sentences. The target is medium-length
sentences with explicit joins, not one long one.

## 3. Definite articles come back

Telegraphic noun phrases become ordinary ones.

- *Left one ran his anomaly tracker.* → *The left one ran his anomaly tracker.*
- *Middle one was a map.* → *The middle one was a map.*

## 4. Simpler diction, and fewer clever constructions

- *the back of her neck told her to* → *her senses told her to*
- *a jacket that fit him better than most teenage boys could manage* → *a jacket that fit him
  perfectly*
- *a permanent half-grin that meant he'd either made or set fire to something* → *…that gave
  off the vibe that he'd either made or set fire to something*

## 5. Implied things get said

This is the largest change and the one with the most consequence. The original often left a
gap for the reader to close; the revision closes it.

- *She didn't remember why.* → *The place was full of good memories.*
- *which Owen wasn't watching but which was making the right noise* → *which Owen was
  half-watching because he put the odds pretty low that he'd ever get it fixed*
- *Felix caught it without looking. They'd been doing this for two years.* → *Felix caught it
  without looking. He never missed because they'd been doing this for two years.*

**Apply this one with care.** It is a real style, but it is the rule most capable of costing
the book something, because the beats the original left implied were usually its best ones.
See the note at the foot.

## 6. Dialogue is warmer and more explicit

- *"Don't make it weird."* → *"Of course! Having you around will liven the place up a bit."*
- *"Come on."* → *"Really? You've got to be kidding me!"*
- *"Yeah. Got that."* → *"Yeah. I think you're right."*
- *"No. You cannot."* → *"No. No you cannot."*

## 7. Mechanics

- **Curly apostrophes and quotes throughout**, `’` and `“ ”`. The prose blocks are set in a
  serif at around 15px, where a straight mark is visibly different. Prompts and production
  notes may use straight ones; the prose may not.
- **Character epithets are only for before a name is known.** See Volume 1 spread 6: the
  narration is close third on Lena, so she cannot use a name she has not been told, but the
  moment a name is spoken aloud the narration uses it and never goes back. If a character
  needs naming, the cheapest fix is four words of dialogue rather than a rewrite.
- **Identical adjacent paragraphs cannot be edited in place.** The save is
  content-addressed, so if the same line appears twice on a page the server cannot tell which
  one you meant and refuses rather than guessing. Ask for the change instead; it is a spec
  edit. Deleting a paragraph is also a spec job, since edit mode can only replace text.
- Page fit is the hard constraint. Every text page must stay under 100% of its column, and the
  cost of prose is **paragraph count** far more than word count, because a one-line paragraph
  occupies a whole line however short it is. Joining fragments therefore usually makes a page
  SHORTER even while adding words, which is why the Volume 1 edits added 43 words to spread 1
  without pushing it over.

## What the revision costs, stated plainly so it can be a choice

Two things went out with the fragments, and both are worth deciding about rather than
discovering later.

**Aunt Carol changed character, not just register.** *"Don't make it weird."* is a prickly,
deadpan woman who is fond of her niece and would never say so. *"Of course! Having you around
will liven the place up a bit."* is a warm one. Both are good; they are not the same person.
Carol appears again in Volume 2 spread 7 and Volume 4 spread 1, both written for the deadpan
version, and one of them is a delivered plate. **Whichever Carol is canon, the other two scenes
need to match her.**

**Shown beats became told ones.** The bag toss is the clearest case. The original was *tossed
the bag at Felix without looking. Felix caught it without looking.* — the mirrored phrase IS
the two years of practice, demonstrated in six words and never explained. The revision keeps
one *without looking* and adds *He never missed because they'd been doing this for two years.*
That is the rule in section 5 doing exactly what it says, and it is the author's call, but when
applying section 5 to Volumes 2 to 4 the question to ask each time is whether the gap was a gap
or a joke.
