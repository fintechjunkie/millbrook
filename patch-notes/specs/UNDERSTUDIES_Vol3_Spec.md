## Shot mix, this volume

Images: 13. Character-led 9, establishing 2, detail 1, animal 1.

**No wide interior of the Warehouse, no crowd, no podium, and nobody addressing a room.** That is
deliberate and it is the volume’s main structural decision. The prose naturally wanted five settings
across thirteen plates with the Warehouse taking five, which would have put arc two at eleven
Warehouse plates out of thirty-six. Three moved out: spread 11 went up onto the roof, spread 12
cropped to two girls on a floor, and spread 4 cut away to the camp on the softball field — a part of
that location no plate had ever shown. **Six of the thirteen carry no location reference at all**,
because an attached reference is authoritative about what is *not* in the room and a tight frame has
nothing to gain from one.

**Two of the four new characters got a plate to themselves rather than sharing one.** Spread 10 was
nearly a split-screen diptych carrying both Sybil and Wren, and it was abandoned: halving the frame
halves the drawn size of both, which is the same failure as putting Wren ninety yards away. Spread
11’s page already contains *"An archer on the roof"* in Milo’s list from the doorway, so Wren took
that slot at full size and Sybil kept spread 10. No prose changed and no fourteenth plate was
needed. What it cost was the wide shot of the whole zoo from Milo’s doorway — which was the last
wide Warehouse interior in the volume, so the trade served both ends at once.

**Two plates are exceptions to the LIGHT rule and both are outdoor night**, spreads 1 and 4, which
is the only case the standing instruction allows. Every interior here is lit as though somebody
switched the lights on, including the two that happen at half past midnight.

**`u3-s09` ships horizontally mirrored.** The delivered frame put the dent, and therefore Felix and
the hair dryer, on Ottie’s LEFT pauldron; it is canonically her RIGHT, two prose lines name that
side, spreads 7 and 8 both draw it there, and Volume 4’s payoff is her carrying that plate under her
arm. Nothing in the frame is canonically left-or-right — no legible text, no asymmetric geometry and
no reference claiming that angle of the Shop — so a flip fixed the side at no cost.
