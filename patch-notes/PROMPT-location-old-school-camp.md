## Attach `public/images/vol3-s04.png` with this prompt

**State A already exists as a delivered plate.** `vol3-s04` is the same school in The Patch,
boarded and empty behind a sagging chain link fence with a hole in the corner. This is the
same building six months later with an army on it, so the building has to survive the change
exactly — same proportions, same brick, same boarded windows, same stripped play structure.

**But the framing deliberately does NOT match, and that is a departure worth naming.**
`MAPLE_AND_FOURTH` A and B must be framed identically, because there the joke IS the framing —
the same corner, twice. Here the joke is what is now *inside* a fence you cannot see through,
and `vol3-s04` is shot from outside at ground level with the fence in the foreground, which
can never show it. So this reference goes up one storey and looks over. **State pair on the
subject, not on the camera.**

It also matches how the arc actually discovers the place: Lena, on the roof of the hardware
store — *“I’m on top of the hardware store and can see the field and the trucks and the tents
they’ve put up already.”*

## The reference exists to protect the break-in

The whole entry turns on one piece of geometry, and it is a joke about a machine that follows
a site plan too literally:

> *“And the property line runs along the outside of this concrete, so that’s where the fence
> went.” Vex pointed at the hatch on the other side of it. “But the boiler hatch is inside.”*

**If the reference runs the fence straight past the dumpster pad, the break-in stops making
sense.** So the pad sits at the near corner of the frame, the fence turns around the outer
edge of the concrete, and the hatch lies flush on the protected side. It is a MUST HOLD.

If any one element has to give in the generation, give up the tennis courts, not the pad.

## The fence is a character

It is not security, it is a copy of a document — *“Nine feet tall. Privacy slats. Green.”*, and
*“Somebody read that page four hours before the trucks got here.”* So it must look **wrong in
the right way**: newer, straighter and better made than the school, the grass or anything else
in frame, with not one sag or gap. Vex’s line depends on it. *“This one isn’t even thinking
about me.”*

## REGENERATION, attempt 2 — the tarp

Attempt 1 is filed and is correct in every other respect: the building matches `vol3-s04`, the
fence is right, the dumpster pad sits inside it with the hatch flush in the slab, six trucks,
five tents, no lettering anywhere. **It delivered the generator and omitted the tarp-covered
object entirely.**

That is not cosmetic. Owen counts the camp off traffic-camera footage — *“There’s one command
tent, four squad tents, a generator, and something under a tarp that isn’t a generator”* — and
he says he counted twice. A reader who counts along finds four of the five things.

**So the two objects are now described separately and at length, and the MUST HOLD says there
are TWO of them.** Everything else in this prompt is unchanged from the version that produced
the good result, so nothing else should move.

## The counts are all stated in the prose and readers will count them

Six trucks. Thirty-one people. **One command tent, four squad tents, a generator, and
something under a tarp that isn’t a generator** — Owen counted them twice off traffic camera
footage. The tarp shape must stay unidentifiable; it is a question the arc asks and does not
answer here.

No figures, so the thirty-one are not in the frame. It is the site, not the scene.
