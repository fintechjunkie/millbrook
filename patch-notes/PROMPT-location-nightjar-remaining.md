## Before you start: check the style on arrival

**The first drive-in attempt satisfied every element of its brief and came back a photograph.**
That had not happened before in this project, and it happened to one generation while five
others in the same batch were correct. So style is now a thing to check on delivery like any
other requirement, and the cheapest check is to open a delivered reference beside the new one —
`loc-old-bell-road.png` is a good yardstick: cut-paper cloud with visible edges, paper texture
in the asphalt.

The drive-in prompt below has the style requirement **hoisted to the head of the prompt**, above
the style block, because the negative block that forbids photographic realism sits at the foot
and the foot is where it was ignored. Nothing else about that prompt has changed.

## The four street references are one system

The Nightjar crosses Millbrook by its roofs, so these four cannot each invent their own town.
Two measurements are load-bearing and both come from the prose rather than from a preference:

**The tower and the feed store.** *“He’d run a line from the tower’s catwalk down to the roof of
the feed store, which is about two hundred feet of cable and a forty-foot drop in the middle of
it.”* He gets ninety feet along it, the far anchor walks, and he hangs **over the middle of the
street** while Vex walks the cable in hand over hand from the catwalk. So: two hundred feet apart,
forty feet of fall, and the line crosses Main Street. Both images show that gap, and neither
shows anything strung across it.

**Carter’s parapet is standable and its back is survivable.** He addresses the whole street from
the parapet of a three-storey building, then leaves *“off the back of the roof in a way that
involved a rope and a bicycle frame, and the sound he made landing was not a good sound”* — and
eleven seconds later there is a thumbs up over the parapet. So the parapet is solid and wide, and
there is a rear elevation with a fire escape and an alley to land badly in.

## Everything the story adds later is deliberately absent

Main Street is generated whole: no hole, no cones, no fresh asphalt, no BEAR CROSSING sign. Part
Three opens a bathtub-sized crater in it and Part Four has the block rebuilt curb to curb.

The feed store roof is bare: no dish, no recorder, no cable, no anchor. He builds a listening post
up there over nine nights and none of it belongs in the room he built it in.

The school is generated EMPTY — the unit left in May, and the flattened rectangles in the grass
where the tents stood are the whole point of the picture.

The drive-in has no cars in it.
