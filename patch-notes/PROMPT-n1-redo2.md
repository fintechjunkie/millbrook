# The Nightjar, Part One: two plates the text change affects

The prose changed on four points this morning. Two of them land on pictures that are already
filed, so those two want another pass. **Everything else in Part One is unaffected.**

## What changed in the text, and why

**The count is eleven, not ninety-one.** It was already eleven everywhere else — Part Three says
*“you came to eleven bedrooms”* and *“eleven for eleven”*, Part Four says *“all eleven of them”*,
and Part One itself says *“eleven people in line”* at the truck and Lena has *“footage of eleven
kids”*. Ninety-one was the outlier and it has gone. It also makes the causation exact: eleven
people were in that line during the hour the warmer was cold, and eleven children were ill.

**They are on Main Street now, and the story says how they got there.** Rosa parks at the little
league field and Carter's Hardware is on Main Street, so the crew could not see a man on that
parapet from a gravel lot. One sentence moves them, and it does a second job: it establishes that
**the six** are the five of the crew plus Rosa, which is what *“he looked down at the six of
them”* has always meant and never said.

**He lands somewhere he could put a hand back over.** He went *“off the back of the roof”* and a
thumbs up appeared over the parapet eleven seconds later — but a parapet is three storeys up and
he was in the alley. He now comes down onto the roof of the building next door, a storey lower,
and the hand comes up over **that** parapet.
