# Canonical reference: the duck

Generated 2026-08-01 from `roster.json`, so STYLE and NEGATIVE are the current blocks
verbatim rather than a copy that can go stale.

## The format is the ANIMAL format, not the character format

**1000x900 on `rgb(234, 214, 185)`, which is 10:9** — not the 3:4 on `rgb(238, 221, 197)`
that every human sheet uses. The pig, Monke and the dragon are all 1000x900 on that ground,
and the duck has to match them, because **all four of them stand in the same room in the same
plate** (Volume 1, spread 6). Four animals on three different grounds at two aspect ratios
would be visible in the composite.

## Your image is a design reference, not a sheet

It is flat cartoon line-and-fill on grey, and it is cropped at the body. So the design is
taken from it — white body, broad yellow bill, single black dot eye, plump and pleased with
itself — and everything else is carried into the project's layered-paper construction and
completed as a full standing figure with legs and webbed feet.

## What the duck is actually for

One joke, and it lands twice:

> *“Pip tilted her head, and everybody in the Warehouse shut up, because they’d learned what
> that meant. **Even the duck.**”* — Volume 1, spread 8

> *“The room went quiet. **Even the duck.**”* — Volume 2

**That is why it stayed a duck.** The line only works because a duck is loud and stupid and
is the last thing in the room you would expect to notice anything. Swapping it for the black
cat kills the joke outright — cats are quiet already — and the croc weakens it, because a
crocodile falling silent is not a change of state. So the design has to read LOUD even
standing still: bill slightly open in a plate, chest out, no self-awareness.

Its only other appearance is Volume 1 spread 6, *“standing in the middle of the floor honking
at nothing”*, which is the plate that establishes the whole menagerie.
