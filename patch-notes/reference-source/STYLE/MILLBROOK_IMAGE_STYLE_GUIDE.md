# MILLBROOK IMAGE STYLE GUIDE

Version 1.1  
Status: Locked visual direction and character canon  
Primary use: Flip-book illustrations, scene art, character sheets, promotional images, and future image-generation prompts for the Millbrook world.

---

## 1. Core Visual Direction

Millbrook is rendered as a **hand-built paper-theater world**.

Every image should feel as though it was assembled from cut, painted, printed, folded, and layered paper pieces on a small theatrical set. Characters, buildings, trees, props, clouds, signs, shadows, and environmental effects all belong to the same crafted physical world.

The result should be:

- whimsical but not childish
- strange but still legible
- tactile and handmade
- slightly imperfect
- expressive enough for comedy, suspense, and character acting
- consistent enough to support a long-running flip-book series

The world should never look like a generic 3D animation, a conventional anime frame, a glossy game render, or a flat digital vector illustration.

---

## 2. The Millbrook Look

### 2.1 Medium

Use a **layered paper-cut diorama / paper puppet theater** aesthetic.

Visible qualities should include:

- cut-paper edges
- slightly fibrous paper texture
- painted or printed surface details
- layered planes with small gaps between them
- soft contact shadows cast by one paper layer onto another
- occasional folded tabs, seams, joins, or articulated puppet connections
- tiny handcrafted irregularities

The materials may resemble:

- matte construction paper
- painted cardstock
- printed scrapbook paper
- weathered book paper
- thin chipboard
- tissue-paper overlays for mist, light, or water
- vellum for windows, reflections, or supernatural effects

The craft should feel intentional and sophisticated, not like a school project.

### 2.2 Dimensionality

Scenes should have depth, but the individual elements remain recognizably flat.

Preferred structure:

1. foreground framing elements
2. primary character plane
3. secondary character or prop plane
4. main environmental plane
5. architectural plane
6. distant town plane
7. sky or atmospheric backing plane

Characters may be assembled from multiple paper pieces, but should still read as paper puppets rather than fully volumetric dolls.

Avoid:

- smooth plastic surfaces
- clay-like characters
- realistic skin rendering
- glossy CGI lighting
- photorealistic depth of field
- inflated or rounded 3D forms

---

## 3. Tone

Millbrook should look welcoming at first glance and subtly wrong on the second.

The visual tone combines:

- cozy small-town familiarity
- dry comedy
- low-level unease
- strange civic history
- handmade charm
- quiet evidence that reality has been patched

The image should not announce the supernatural unless the scene calls for it. Ordinary places should contain one or two details that are slightly off.

Examples:

- a window that does not align with the floor
- a fountain plaque installed slightly crooked
- one tree casting the wrong-shaped shadow
- two identical houses with one impossible difference
- a street that narrows too quickly
- a shop that appears older than the buildings around it
- repeated objects with tiny mismatches
- a paper seam visible where the sky seems to have been replaced

Do not overload every scene with surreal details. Millbrook works best when the strange detail is specific and confidently presented.

---

## 4. Character Design System

### 4.1 General Proportions

Characters must remain immediately recognizable in full-body form.

Use:

- slightly oversized heads
- large, readable eyes
- clear facial silhouettes
- long enough limbs for expressive posing
- slightly enlarged hands and shoes for gesture clarity
- compact torsos
- distinctive hair, hats, glasses, and clothing shapes

Avoid chibi proportions. Characters should feel like teenagers, not toddlers.

Recommended full-body proportion:

- approximately 5 to 5.5 heads tall
- hands slightly oversized
- feet large enough to anchor the paper puppet visually

### 4.2 Paper-Puppet Construction

A character may be built from separate pieces for:

- head
- hair front and back
- neck
- torso
- upper and lower arms
- hands
- upper and lower legs
- shoes
- clothing overlays
- accessories
- facial expression pieces

Joints can be hidden under clothing overlaps or shown subtly as crafted pivots. Do not make characters look like wooden marionettes unless specifically requested.

### 4.3 Faces

Faces should be simple, expressive, and readable at small size.

Use:

- large eyes with clear gaze direction
- restrained nose shapes
- simple mouth shapes
- visible freckles and key identifying marks
- eyebrows or equivalent expression cues when character canon permits

Expressions should be specific rather than generic. Millbrook humor often depends on one character reacting while another remains completely unbothered.

### 4.4 Silhouette Rule

Each main character should be identifiable in solid silhouette through at least three of the following:

- hair shape
- hat or glasses
- jacket or shirt shape
- body posture
- signature accessory
- color grouping

Never rely on facial details alone for recognition.

## 6. Town Design

### 6.1 Architecture

Millbrook is an old American small town assembled over many decades and patched repeatedly.

Use:

- brick storefronts
- narrow townhouses
- civic stonework
- clock towers
- old libraries
- hardware stores
- laundromats
- alleys
- water towers
- modest houses with porches
- municipal benches, lamps, plaques, trash cans, and signs

Buildings should have personality. Rooflines may lean slightly. Windows may be uneven. Additions may not match the original structure.

Avoid a spotless theme-park town. Millbrook should feel lived in, repaired, painted over, and locally maintained.

### 6.2 Environmental Distortion

Surrealism should emerge from the paper-world construction and the town's unstable reality.

Acceptable distortions:

- forced perspective that becomes slightly impossible
- buildings stacked on visibly separate paper planes
- a street curling upward like a strip of paper
- clouds attached to thin visible supports
- a tree canopy shaped like a theatrical curtain
- windows glowing through vellum
- an alley folded like an accordion
- missing architectural pieces shown as clean paper gaps
- duplicated scenery with one altered detail
- a patch of sky that is visibly a different paper stock

The location must remain recognizable. A surreal park must still read as the Millbrook park. A surreal Main Street must still function as Main Street.

## 7. Color

### 7.1 Overall Palette

Use a warm, muted, storybook palette with concentrated character accents.

Town colors:

- weathered brick red
- faded teal
- moss and olive greens
- warm stone
- parchment
- dusty blue
- charcoal
- muted mustard
- dull municipal green

Character colors should remain stronger and more identifiable than the environment.

### 7.2 Color Behavior

- Keep saturation controlled.
- Preserve signature character colors.
- Use one or two brighter accents to guide the eye.
- Avoid rainbow palettes unless the scene specifically requires them.
- Blacks should usually read as charcoal, ink, or very dark plum rather than digitally pure black.
- Whites should read as warm paper or cream rather than screen white.

### 7.3 Supernatural Color

Reality patches and anomalies may introduce colors not otherwise present, especially:

- electric violet
- unnatural cyan
- acidic green
- deep magenta
- overexposed paper white

Use these sparingly. An anomaly color should feel like a foreign material inserted into the crafted world.

---

## 8. Lighting

Lighting should reinforce the miniature paper-theater construction.

Use:

- soft directional studio light
- gentle cast shadows between layers
- warm late-afternoon light
- cool moonlit paper blues
- localized practical light from lamps or windows
- translucent paper glow for supernatural effects

The paper layers should cast visible but soft-edged shadows. Lighting may be theatrical, but should not become glossy or cinematic CGI.

Avoid:

- harsh photorealistic skin highlights
- neon rim lighting on every subject
- excessive bloom
- deep black crushed shadows that hide the paper construction

---

## 9. Composition for Flip-Book Scenes

### 9.1 Readability

Every scene should have a clear primary action.

Priorities:

1. character identity
2. body language
3. key prop or anomaly
4. location recognition
5. supporting town detail

Do not let background craftsmanship compete with the story beat.

### 9.2 Staging

Favor:

- clear full-body or three-quarter-body staging
- theatrical left-to-right blocking
- readable overlaps
- strong negative space around faces and hands
- distinct planes for separate characters
- poses that can be continued from frame to frame

Avoid tangents where limbs merge into scenery or characters overlap so heavily that their silhouettes disappear.

### 9.3 Camera

Default camera language:

- eye-level or slightly low theatrical viewpoint
- straight-on or gently angled stage composition
- moderate depth
- no extreme fisheye unless motivated by an anomaly
- no generic portrait crop when the scene requires body acting

For style-comparison tests, keep framing, poses, expressions, and character placement identical across variants.

---

## 10. Detail Density

Images should reward a second look without becoming cluttered.

Include small narrative details such as:

- crooked plaques
- mismatched buttons
- taped repairs
- handwritten labels
- tiny printed patterns
- worn bench slats
- civic notices
- old shop signs
- misplaced shadows
- one repeated object with a changed feature

Do not fill every surface. Preserve calm areas so the characters remain dominant.

---

## 11. Text and Signage

Text may appear when it belongs naturally in the scene, but generated text is unreliable and should be tightly controlled.

Preferred approach:

- keep signs short
- use one to four words where possible
- use legible block lettering
- treat longer text as texture unless it will be added manually later
- reserve exact dialogue, chapter headings, and important copy for post-production

Recurring place names should be spelled correctly:

- MILLBROOK
- MAIN STREET
- HOLLOW PINE BOOKS
- TOWN HALL
- PUBLIC LIBRARY

Do not invent dates, slogans, or civic history unless required by the source material.

---

## 12. Animation and Continuity Rules

For flip-book sequences:

- preserve character scale from frame to frame
- preserve clothing construction and paper textures
- keep the same joint placement
- preserve prop size and hand placement
- maintain the same environmental paper planes whenever the camera does not move
- change only what the action requires
- keep lighting direction stable
- retain small town imperfections across consecutive frames

Create and reuse character turnaround sheets for:

- front
- profile
- three-quarter front
- three-quarter back
- back
- seated pose
- walking pose
- running pose
- neutral hands
- key expressions

## 13. Prompt Construction Standard

Every future image prompt should include the following blocks in this order.

### A. Style block

> A sophisticated layered paper-cut diorama and articulated paper-puppet illustration. Every character, building, prop, plant, cloud, and atmospheric effect is made from painted, printed, folded, or cut matte paper. Visible fibrous paper texture, handcrafted cut edges, overlapping flat planes, and soft contact shadows between layers. The scene feels like a meticulously built miniature paper theater, not CGI, not clay, not plastic, and not a flat vector drawing.

### B. Tone block

> Whimsical, warm, and inviting at first glance, with one or two subtle visual details that make the ordinary small town feel slightly wrong. Dry comedy and low-level mystery rather than horror.

### C. Character block

List every visible character using the canonical anchors from Section 5. Include full-body pose, expression, action, gaze direction, and signature object.

### D. Scene block

Describe:

- exact location
- source-story action
- character placement
- important props
- recognizable Millbrook architecture
- one restrained surreal or anomalous detail

### E. Composition block

Specify:

- aspect ratio
- camera height
- shot size
- character arrangement
- visual priority
- required empty space for captions, if any

### F. Negative block

> No photorealism, no glossy 3D render, no plastic toy appearance, no claymation, no anime screencap, no smooth vector art, no painterly realism, no chibi proportions, no generic fantasy village, no illegible character silhouettes, no cropped feet when full body is requested, no extra limbs, no duplicate characters, and no unrequested costume changes.

## 15. Quality-Control Checklist

Before approving an image, confirm all of the following.

### Style

- [ ] The entire scene reads as a paper-built world.
- [ ] Cut edges and paper texture are visible.
- [ ] Layered planes and contact shadows create depth.
- [ ] Nothing important looks plastic, clay-like, or glossy CGI.

### Characters

- [ ] Every named character is immediately recognizable.
- [ ] Canonical hair, clothing, accessories, and skin tones are preserved.
- [ ] Full-body proportions are consistent.
- [ ] Body language communicates the story beat.
- [ ] Hands, feet, and facial features are structurally sound.

### Millbrook

- [ ] The location is recognizable.
- [ ] The town feels old, lived in, and locally repaired.
- [ ] At least one detail is subtly strange.
- [ ] The surreal element does not erase the function of the setting.

### Storytelling

- [ ] The primary action reads instantly.
- [ ] The viewer knows where to look first.
- [ ] The key prop or anomaly is visible.
- [ ] Background details support rather than overpower the scene.

### Continuity

- [ ] Wardrobe matches adjacent scenes.
- [ ] Character scale is consistent.
- [ ] Repeated props and locations retain their construction.
- [ ] Lighting direction and environmental planes remain stable.

This is the default style for all Millbrook image generation unless a project brief explicitly overrides it.
