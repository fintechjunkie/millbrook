# u3-s10 and u3-s11 — Sybil, and Wren on the roof

Two full 3:2 plates, one per spread. This replaces an earlier draft that tried to fit both beats
into spread 10 as a split-screen diptych.

**Why not the diptych.** Spread 10's page carries two of Part Three's best beats in two different
places at once — Wren being right about everything, and Sybil turning off every machine in the
building with one finger — and picking one appeared to lose the other. But halving the frame halves
the drawn size of both figures, which is the same problem as putting Wren ninety yards away: you
stop being able to see the features of a character the reader has only just met. A diptych would
also be the only one in fifty-one plates.

**Why not a recut either.** Giving Sybil her own spread means five pages out of spreads 9 to 12's
808 words, so about 162 each. That is legal — this volume already has a 158 and a 167 — but it
moves four page boundaries, and a moved boundary changes which beat each plate may depict and forces
every affected spoiler check to be re-read by hand. It also adds a fourteenth plate and changes the
roster's production arithmetic.

**What actually solves it, at no cost.** Spread 11's page already contains Wren on the roof, in
Milo's list from the doorway: *"A boxer losing a video game with tremendous dignity. An archer on
the roof. A witch apologizing to a router."* So Wren's plate moves to spread 11 and Sybil keeps
spread 10 to herself. No prose changes, the volume stays at thirteen plates, and both new
characters are drawn full size.

What it displaces is the wide shot of the whole zoo from Milo's doorway — **which was the last wide
Warehouse interior in the volume**, so the trade also serves the standing instruction about not
returning to that room. Nothing in Milo's list goes unillustrated: the knight with the hair dryer is
spread 9 and the witch with the router is spread 10.

**The location reference is a plainer version of this moment, so the prompt carries the whole
difference.** `loc-terminals.png` has nobody in it and every screen intact; this beat has two people
in it and every screen in the building dead. Both facts are stated below rather than left to the
scene's logic — the failure that returned an empty podium and empty chairs from `loc-square.png`.

**Why the instant after and not the one before.** The funny frame is the second *afterwards*: every
screen black, the finger still resting on the trackpad, and the twelve-year-old in the pointed hat
mildly sorry about it. A finger about to land is only a girl touching a laptop.

**Play her age straight.** Her roster block is explicit — the lines were written for an older woman
and land funnier delivered by a child, and the joke is that she does not appear to notice she is
twelve. Not smug, not mischievous, not winking at the reader. Politely apologetic, exactly as an
aunt would be about a dropped plate.

## [ ] `u3-s11.png` · Wren on the roof · aspect **3:2**

Shot type: Character close, single figure, exterior

Depicts: beat 1, from Milo's list — *"An archer on the roof."* Around six in the evening.

Spoiler check: PASS. The final beat of the page is *"Item forty-one. Nineteen sixty-eight. A child's
coat, black wool, three orange buttons, size six."* This plate is on the roof, four hours and one
scene earlier, with nobody else in frame.

**No location token, deliberately.** The only reference for this building is a street-level exterior
with no roof in it, and an attached reference is authoritative about what is *not* there. The roof
is described in full instead.

**She must be CLOSE.** This is the correction the author made on `u2-s08` and it applies with more
force here: at a distance you see none of her features, which defeats the purpose of introducing
her. The roof is the setting, not the subject.

**Six in the evening is still a light picture.** Low warm sun, long shadows, everything readable.
Not dusk, not silhouette, not a figure against a sunset.

## If the roof plate does not work

The fallback is the wide shot of the whole zoo from Milo's doorway, which is what spread 11 was
originally going to get, and Wren then has no plate in the volume. That is the reason to get this
one right rather than to hedge: she and Sybil are both introduced here, and Rocco and Ottie already
have spread 9.
