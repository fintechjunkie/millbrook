Ten plates delivered 2026-08-07, all 1536x1024 or 1774x887, all correct aspect, all opaque, all
in the paper style. **Four are finished. Four want a small modification rather than a new
generation. Two want regenerating**, and both of those were generated before the prompt that
would have prevented the fault existed.

The order below is the order I would do them in: the four edits are quick and low-risk, and the
two regenerations are the only ones that can come back worse than what you already have.

## Modify — keep the picture, change one thing

These four are right in every respect except the one named. **Edit them rather than regenerating:
each is a local change and a new generation would put everything else at risk.**

### `n1-s04` — Milo on the bathroom floor · GREEN

Everything else is correct: he is on the floor against the tub with the beanie and the red lenses
still on, his mother is on the rim mid-anecdote with one hand up, and nobody is touching anybody.

> Give Milo's face, neck and hands a distinct sickly GREEN cast over his own skin tone — a pale,
> faintly grey-green pallor across the forehead, cheeks and around the mouth, unmistakable at a
> glance and not subtle. He is wrung out, not comical. Change nothing else in the picture.

### `n1-s05` — the nine-year-old sitting up in bed · GREEN

Everything else is correct: the doorway reads as a hole in a flat wall, Chet is mid-stride through
it at the right height, and the boy is astonished rather than frightened.

> Give the boy's face and hands a distinct sickly GREEN cast over his own skin tone — a pale
> grey-green pallor across the forehead and cheeks, unmistakable at a glance. He is ill and has
> not been fixed yet. Change nothing else in the picture.

### `n1-s06` — Felix asleep in front of the television · GREEN

**This is the best plate in the batch and the most important image in the arc.** The screen is
exactly right: a masked man on a wet rooftop in the rain, grainy and blue, with no text anywhere
on it. Chet reads correctly small. Do not regenerate this one.

> Over Felix's deep brown skin, lay a dulled ashen olive-green across the face and the backs of
> the hands — the healthy warmth drained out of the brown and a green-grey laid over it. **Do not
> lighten his skin and do not change his race**: he is the same dark-skinned boy, unwell. Change
> nothing else in the picture.

### `n1-s01` — the truck at golden hour · TWO IDENTITY SLIPS

The staging is right — Vex's boot is on the bumper with an armful of food, Rosa is out of the
hatch counting on her fingers, Owen has a hand out for his order, the generator is running at the
back. Two locked features slipped, and both are on the roster's list of things that are never
negotiable whatever anyone is wearing.

> 1. Vex's mint-green hair must read as **TWO messy buns, one high on each side of her head**, not
>    as a single gathered spiky mass. Two distinct bunches, clearly separated, one either side.
> 2. Owen's eyebrows must be **strong and dark** against his very pale skin and bald head. They are
>    currently far too faint. They are his most identifying feature.
>
> Change nothing else in the picture.

## Regenerate — the prompt has changed since these were made

### `n1-s02` — Pip and Rosa at the hatch

**This is the one you flagged, and you were right.** Rosa's head sits well above Pip's and is
drawn larger, so she reads as a bigger-framed person. She is not.

The prompt now carries an explicit SCALE paragraph that did not exist when this was generated:
Pip is the tallest of the three girls, so **on a shared floor Pip would be very slightly the
taller of the two**, and Rosa is raised here only by the height of the truck floor she stands on.
It also says Rosa must not be drawn broad, heavy, matronly or larger-framed than Pip.

Everything else about the delivered image is lovely — the plate with one bite gone, Rosa's
astonishment, Pip's face doing nothing — so it is worth keeping as the composition to aim at.

### `n1-s08` — the Nightjar on the parapet

Two faults, and the second is the one that matters.

**The building does not read as three storeys.** He is standing on what looks like a low wall at
roughly the camera's own level, with the two kids on the roadway just below him. The prose is
specific: *“three stories up”*, and the whole comedy of the scene is Owen shouting up at a ledge
he has seen the inspection for.

**He is large, near, and his face is fully readable.** The prompt asks for a wide low-angle shot
with him small and dark against a bright open sky, features not readable, and this is the
opposite. That staging is not fussiness: the roster records the decision that Part One keeps him
far and later volumes come closer, so the reader's certainty grows on the same curve as Pip's
investigation.

**Worth saying plainly: the delivered image is good, and there is a real argument for it.** The
costume is perfect — teal jacket, charcoal cape, flat cap, red-orange sneakers, opaque white
goggles — and seeing him clearly on his first appearance turns Part One into dramatic irony
rather than a puzzle, which is the direction you already chose when you kept Felix's face under
the mask. **If you would rather have him close, say so and I will change the prompt and the
roster note instead of the picture.** But the three-storey problem needs fixing either way.

## One thing I am not sure about, and it is yours to call

**The opener is softer than everything else in the set.** It has paper grain and flat layered
forms, and it is plainly not photographic — but its foliage and rooftops are painted rather than
cut, where `loc-old-bell-road.png` and `n1-s09` have hard visible paper edges. At a 2:1 panorama
the individual cuts would be tiny, so this may simply be what the style does at that scale.

It is the only full-bleed image in the volume and it runs across both pages, so it is the one
worth being fussy about. Compare it against `n1-s09` at full size and decide.
